Series A update · Q3

Revenue is up, the product is stable, and we need one hire.

Seven slides: what changed, the evidence behind it, and the decision we are asking for today.

Presented to the board, 12 October.

We came into the quarter with a clear constraint: the support queue.

  • Two enterprise accounts were waiting on onboarding.
  • Support response time was the top churn reason.
  • We chose to fix onboarding before adding seats.

Source: churn interviews, June.

We rebuilt onboarding as a guided checklist and cut the queue.

  • Self-serve import replaced two manual calls per account.
  • Checklist completion is now visible to the customer.
  • The support queue is below one day for the first time.

Retention and activation improved together, not one at the cost of the other.

Activation: 61% of new accounts finish onboarding in week one.

Net revenue retention held flat while new accounts grew. No discounting was used to get there.

Source: product analytics, last 90 days.

Each new account now pays back in the first quarter.

  • Support cost per account is down by a third.
  • Onboarding labour is now one hour, not five.
  • Payback period: 2.4 months, blended.

Source: finance model v6, reviewed by the board.

Next two quarters: one senior support engineer, then pricing work.

  • Q4: hire one support engineer and document onboarding.
  • Q1: move to usage-based pricing for larger accounts.
  • No other headcount changes.

The ask: approve the support engineer and the Q1 pricing work.

Approval today keeps the Q4 start date. We will bring the pricing model back for review in January.

Decision requested by 31 October.

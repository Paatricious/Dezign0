Read the dune before the storm.

Marram turns a seasonal transect into a repeatable field record — so restoration crews argue about plants, not spreadsheets.

Prepared by R. Vale14 March 2026Field programme

01 — The problem

The dune is moving. The record of it is not.

Crews walk the same transect four times a year. Each walk produces a folder of photographs, a marked-up map and a spreadsheet that only one person knows how to read.

  • Three formats, one afternoonPhotos live on a phone, sketch marks on paper, numbers in a sheet. Joining them costs the rest of the day.
  • The comparison never happensA transect is only useful against last season's. Without a shared record, every survey starts from zero.
  • Handover is oralWhen the crew lead changes, the reasoning behind a planting decision leaves with them.

02 — The system

One walk, three artefacts.

Capture

Pinned observations

Each photo carries a stake number, a bearing and a time. No free-floating images, no guessing where a picture was taken.

Compare

Season over season

The same stake is shown against its previous three visits, with the change in surface height stated in centimetres.

Hand over

A briefing, not a dump

One page per transect: what changed, what was planted, what to check next. Printable, and short enough to read in the truck.
